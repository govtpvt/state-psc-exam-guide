State Public Service Commission Exams: How They Work

Every Indian state has a Public Service Commission that recruits officers for the state government: administrative, police, revenue, finance and many departmental services. The exams resemble the UPSC civil services exam in shape, but each state writes its own rules. This guide explains what is common and what to check for your state. State notifications are listed with others on the Govtpvt latest government jobs page.

What a state PSC recruits for

  • Combined state services: deputy collector, deputy superintendent of police, tax and revenue officers, block development officers and similar posts.
  • Departmental posts: assistant engineers, medical officers, lecturers, agriculture and forest officers.
  • Subordinate and ministerial posts in states where the PSC, and not a separate selection board, handles them.
  • State eligibility tests for assistant professors in some states, such as the Rajasthan SET.

The three stages

StageFormatCounts for merit?
PreliminaryObjective papers on general studies and, in many states, aptitudeNo, screening only
MainWritten descriptive papers, with a state-specific paper or language paperYes
InterviewPersonality test before a boardYes, added to main marks

Some states have moved parts of the main exam to objective format, and the number of papers varies. The current pattern is always in the notification.

What changes from state to state

Age limits, the number of attempts, domicile-based reservation, language requirements and the weight of state-specific topics. These are covered on the state rules page.

Preparing alongside other exams

The general studies syllabus overlaps heavily with UPSC and with other states, so one base preparation serves several exams. What differs is the state paper: history, geography, economy and current affairs of that state. See preparing the state-specific paper.
