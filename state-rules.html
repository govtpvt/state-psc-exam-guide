Domicile, Age and Language Rules in State PSC Exams

Candidates often prepare for a neighbouring state's exam and discover late that they are treated differently from local candidates. These are the rules to read before you apply outside your home state.

Domicile and reservation

Most states allow candidates from anywhere in India to apply, but reservation for SC, ST, OBC and EWS categories is normally limited to those domiciled in the state. Candidates from other states compete in the unreserved category. Horizontal reservation for women is also restricted to residents in several states.

Age limits

The upper age limit differs widely between states and between services in the same exam; uniformed posts usually have a lower limit. Age relaxations are often available only to residents. Check the date on which age is counted, since it is not the same in every notification.

Number of attempts

Some commissions cap attempts, some do not, and the count may differ by category. The notification states whether appearing in the preliminary exam counts as an attempt.

Language

  • A qualifying paper in the state language is common in the main exam.
  • Several states require proof of having studied the language up to a given class, or a separate proficiency test before appointment.
  • The medium in which you may write answers is specified; it is not always both English and the state language for every paper.

Physical standards

Police, excise, forest and transport posts within the combined exam carry height, chest and fitness standards. If you do not meet them, leave those posts out of your preferences.

A checklist before applying

  1. Am I eligible as a non-resident, and in which category will I be counted?
  2. Do I meet the age limit for that category on the cut-off date?
  3. Is there a language requirement I cannot meet?
  4. How many vacancies are there in the category I will be counted in?
