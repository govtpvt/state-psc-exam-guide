Preparing the State-Specific Paper for a PSC Exam

The state-specific portion is where a state PSC exam is usually won or lost, because every serious candidate has covered the common general studies syllabus. It is also the portion for which good material is hardest to find. Official documents fill most of the gap.

What the paper covers

  • History, culture, literature and prominent personalities of the state
  • Geography: rivers, soils, climate, minerals, districts and divisions
  • Economy: agriculture, industry, budget and major schemes
  • Administration: state legislature, local bodies, commissions
  • Current affairs of the state

Sources that are free and reliable

SourceUse it for
State economic survey or reviewEconomy, sector data, scheme outcomes
State budget speech and budget at a glancePriorities, new schemes, figures examiners like
State board textbooks for classes 6 to 12History, geography and culture at the right depth
Government press releases and the state gazetteCurrent affairs and new policies
Census and statistical handbookPopulation, literacy, district facts

Method

  1. Print the syllabus of the state paper and mark each line as you cover it.
  2. Collect the last five papers and note which sub-topics repeat.
  3. Make one page of facts per district and per major scheme; revise these weekly.
  4. For the main exam, practise writing 150 to 250 word answers that use state examples and data.

After the exam

State commissions publish provisional keys and invite objections, often with a fee per question. Check the answer keys page and then the results page for the cut-off and the main exam schedule.
